## How to Use This Checklist

This compliance checklist is designed to serve as a working document for facility managers responsible for emergency generator systems. It covers the inspection, testing, maintenance, and documentation requirements established by NFPA 110 (Standard for Emergency and Standby Power Systems), the EPA RICE NESHAP (40 CFR Part 63, Subpart ZZZZ), the stationary diesel engine NSPS (40 CFR Part 60, Subpart IIII), and common AHJ expectations.

Where an item comes directly from a code or regulation, the reference column cites it. NFPA 110 section numbers are from the 2010 edition, which CMS enforces for healthcare facilities through NFPA 99-2012; numbering can differ in the edition your AHJ has adopted (the current edition is 2025). Items marked "Manufacturer" or "Best practice" are not specific code requirements but are part of a sound maintenance program. For the full testing requirements, see our [NFPA 110 compliance guide](/generators/nfpa-110-emergency-generator-compliance-guide). Use this checklist in three ways:

1. **As a routine audit tool** to verify your ongoing maintenance program covers all required items
2. **As an inspection preparation guide** when you know an AHJ visit is scheduled
3. **As a gap analysis** to identify areas where your current program may be deficient

Items are organized by inspection frequency. The status column uses the following indicators:

- **Pass** = Item meets requirements
- **Fail** = Item does not meet requirements and needs corrective action
- **N/A** = Item does not apply to this installation

## Monthly Exercise and Testing Checklist

NFPA 110 requires diesel generator sets to be exercised under load at least monthly for at least 30 minutes (Section 8.4.2). Healthcare facilities must run the monthly test 12 times a year at intervals of not less than 20 days and not more than 40 days. This checklist covers the exercise test and associated monthly inspections.

<Callout type="warning" title="Important">
If your diesel generator's monthly tests do not reach 30% of nameplate kW or the manufacturer's minimum exhaust gas temperature, NFPA 110 still requires monthly exercise with the available load, plus an annual supplemental load test: at least 30 minutes at 50% of nameplate followed by at least 60 minutes at 75% (Section 8.4.2.3). Consistent light-load testing without the supplemental test is a common finding.
</Callout>

## Red Flag Items: Common Causes of Failed Inspections

The following items are common reasons generators draw findings during inspections. Address these proactively to avoid citations.

### Critical Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Fails | How to Fix It |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **No monthly test records** | NFPA 110 Section 8.4.2 requires monthly exercise, and missing records are usually treated as missing tests | Implement monthly testing immediately; never backfill records |
| **Light-load testing without a supplemental test** | Monthly tests below 30% of nameplate (or the manufacturer's minimum exhaust temperature) require an annual supplemental load test under Section 8.4.2.3 | Arrange for a portable load bank for the annual test, or add load to monthly tests |
| **Dead or weak starting batteries** | Batteries are a frequent cause of failure to start, and inspectors may witness a start attempt | Inspect and test batteries on the NFPA 110 schedule; replace based on test results and age |
| **No supplemental or 36-month test report** | Where required, the annual supplemental test (8.4.2.3) and the Level 1 36-month, 4-hour test (8.4.9) must be documented | Schedule and perform the test; retain the detailed report |
| **Missing operations log** | Inspectors expect a chronological record of every operating event | Start a log immediately; maintain it consistently going forward |
| **Block heater not functioning** | A cold engine may not start and accept load within 10 seconds on a Type 10 system | Check block heater operation weekly; repair immediately if failed |
| **Fuel quality not tested** | NFPA 110 requires an annual fuel quality test, and degraded fuel causes filter clogging and starting problems | Test fuel annually using ASTM standards or the manufacturer's recommendations; remediate if quality is marginal |
| **Transfer switch not fully tested** | Transfer switches must be operated monthly; many facilities skip retransfer verification | Include the full transfer and retransfer cycle in every monthly test |
| **EPA hour tracking absent** | Hour records separating emergency and non-emergency operation are required, and without them you cannot show the engine qualifies as emergency | Record hours from the hour meter and classify each operating event |
| **Stored materials in generator room** | NFPA 110 does not permit the EPS room to be used for unrelated purposes, and combustible storage is a fire hazard | Remove all non-essential items; post signage prohibiting storage |

<Callout type="danger" title="Immediate Action Required">
If any of the critical red flag items above apply to your facility, address them before your next AHJ inspection. Multiple findings in a single inspection can lead to follow-up inspections, increased scrutiny, and in some jurisdictions, fines.
</Callout>

## How to Prepare for an AHJ Inspection

When you know an AHJ inspection is approaching, or as part of your routine annual preparation, follow this structured approach to maximize your chances of a clean inspection.

### 30 Days Before the Inspection

1. **Complete your annual maintenance.** Perform the annual service, including any required supplemental load test, before the inspection date. Having fresh service records demonstrates active compliance.

2. **Audit your documentation.** Walk through the Documentation Readiness Checklist above. Identify and fill any gaps. Organize records chronologically and make them easy to navigate.

3. **Conduct a self-inspection.** Use the weekly, monthly, quarterly, and annual checklists in this guide to perform a thorough self-assessment. Document any deficiencies found and the corrective actions taken.

4. **Verify all signage.** Confirm that generator room signage, emergency shutdown labels, and hazard warnings are in place, legible, and compliant with local requirements.

5. **Clean the generator area.** Remove any stored items, sweep the floor, clean up oil drips, and ensure the space looks well-maintained. First impressions set the tone for the entire inspection.

### The Day of the Inspection

1. **Have documentation ready.** Print or prepare digital access to your operations log, maintenance log, test records, fuel records, and permits. Do not make the inspector wait while you search for records.

2. **Assign an escort.** Designate a knowledgeable staff member to accompany the inspector. This person should be able to explain your maintenance program, answer questions about operating history, and demonstrate generator operation if requested.

3. **Be prepared for a witnessed start.** Some inspectors will want to see the generator start and the transfer switch operate. Verify that the system is in automatic mode and ready for a live demonstration.

4. **Know your system.** Be prepared to state the generator's kW rating, voltage configuration, fuel type, EPA certification, and installation date. Know the nameplate details of your transfer switch.

5. **Do not argue findings.** If the inspector identifies a deficiency, acknowledge it professionally and ask for clarification on the required corrective action. Request the finding in writing with the specific code reference. Disputes are resolved through the appeals process, not during the inspection.

### After the Inspection

1. **Review the inspection report** carefully for accuracy and completeness.
2. **Develop a corrective action plan** for any cited deficiencies, with specific timelines and responsible parties.
3. **Complete corrective actions** before the deadline specified by the AHJ.
4. **Document the corrective actions** taken, including photographs where applicable.
5. **Submit documentation** of corrective actions to the AHJ if required.
6. **Update your maintenance program** to prevent recurrence of any cited deficiencies.

## Maintaining Ongoing Compliance

Compliance is not a once-a-year event that coincides with your AHJ inspection. It is the result of a disciplined, year-round program that integrates inspection, testing, maintenance, and documentation into your regular facility management operations. Our [weekly, monthly, and annual maintenance schedule](/generators/weekly-monthly-annual-generator-maintenance-schedule) and [printable NFPA 110 test log](/generators/printable-nfpa-110-generator-test-log) can help.

The checklists in this guide provide the framework. Your job as a facility manager is to assign responsibility, establish accountability, and verify execution. When your program runs consistently throughout the year, AHJ inspections become confirmations of what you already know: your emergency power system is compliant, well-maintained, and ready to protect your facility and its occupants.
